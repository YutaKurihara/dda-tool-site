Forest fire — VIIRS active-fire detection

Thermal detection of burning, read from the satellite feed for all 514 Kabupaten/Kota every day and split by forest cover (03_dashboard/02_forestfire.js). Unlike the other three hazards there is no Stage-1 detector and no stored result: a fire season is a stream that updates daily, not an event with a footprint.

1 · Detection logic

Optical sensors see smoke; thermal sensors see the fire beneath it. That is decisive in Indonesia, where the fire season is the cloud season.

  1. Image selection — VIIRS near-real-time active fire at 375 m, from both platforms merged: NASA/LANCE/SNPP_VIIRS/C2 and NASA/LANCE/NOAA20_VIIRS/C2, filtered to Indonesia. Two satellites roughly double the overpasses per day.
  2. Detection — each image is a daily raster; a pixel with Bright_ti4 > 0 registered a thermal anomaly bright enough to be flagged as fire.
  3. Accumulation — over the selected period, every pixel that fired at least once is kept. A pixel burning for five days running is one burned pixel, not five.
  4. Forest test — a burned pixel counts as forest when the in-house Random-Forest land-cover map (2025, 30 m) says forest at that pixel's centre.

2 · From detections to burned area

One VIIRS pixel is 375 m × 375 m = 0.140625 km². Burned area is the count of distinct detected pixels times that constant:

$$\text{burned area}\;[\mathrm{km^2}] \;=\; N_{\text{distinct pixels}} \;\times\; 0.140625$$

Each city gets two figures — total burned area and the part of it that was forest — plus the share of its own forest that has gone. The forest denominator is sampled rather than summed: one 30 m sample per 1 km cell, which is unbiased for an area total at a fraction of the cost of reading every pixel of the country.

4 · Limits to keep in mind

This is a hotspot footprint, not a burn perimeter. It over-states a small fire — one smouldering hectare lights a whole 375 m pixel — and under-states a large one whose scar sat under cloud at every overpass and was never detected. It is a consistent daily indicator of where fire is concentrated, and should not be quoted as mapped burned area.
  • No burn severity. Sentinel-2 dNBR needs a clear pre/post pair; over a month-long window in Kalimantan the composite is mostly holes and the "low severity" class fills with ordinary vegetation change.
  • Small Kabupaten carry sampling noise in the forest-share column, as a consequence of the 1 km sampling above.
  • The feed runs behind. Daily charts stop at the last day LANCE has published, not at today — a trailing zero would read as "the fires stopped" rather than "nobody has looked yet".

5 · Display and outputs

Hotspots are coloured by recency, newest first: violet → red → orange → yellow. In the live window the classes are under 1 day, 1–3, 3–7 and 7 days or older; within a past month the same four colours split it into quarters. Violet leads because it is the one hue absent from an optical scene of Indonesia — red would lose fresh detections against the red-brown burn scar and bare peat that surround them.

One period control drives the map, the KPI row, the province ranking and all three charts together — daily within the period, monthly across the year, yearly across the last six. Clicking a Kabupaten/Kota re-scopes every chart to it. The table ranks 34 provinces rather than 514 cities; every city is in the CSV export.
