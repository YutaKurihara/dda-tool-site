Forest fire — VIIRS active-fire detection

Thermal detection of burning, read from the satellite feed for all 514 Kabupaten/Kota every day and split by forest cover (03_dashboard/02_forestfire.js). This screen is a stream, not an event: it updates daily, stores nothing, and puts no money on anything.

Two scripts, two different measurements. Fire is the one hazard here mapped twice, because "where is it burning today" and "what finally burned" are different questions with different sensors. This page describes both. The daily thermal monitor (§1–4) reads the VIIRS feed and stores nothing. Its sibling, 02_event/05_burnscar_detection.js (§5), maps the perimeter after the fact from a Sentinel-1 radar time series at 30 m and reports burned hectares per Kabupaten/Kota. The two will not agree, and are not meant to. A single smouldering hectare lights a whole 375 m thermal cell, all 14 ha of it, so the monitor reads several times larger than the scar. Use the monitor for situational awareness during the season, and the scar for the area figure afterwards. Neither puts a rupiah value on a fire: burned area is the reported quantity.

1 · Detection logic

Optical sensors see smoke; thermal sensors see the fire beneath it. That is decisive in Indonesia, where the fire season is the cloud season.

  1. Image selection — VIIRS near-real-time active fire at 375 m, from both platforms merged: NASA/LANCE/SNPP_VIIRS/C2 and NASA/LANCE/NOAA20_VIIRS/C2, filtered to Indonesia. Two satellites roughly double the overpasses per day.
  2. Detection — each image is a daily raster. A pixel counts when Bright_ti4 > 0 and the detection is of nominal or high confidence. Dropping the low-confidence class matters: that is where gas flares, hot industrial roofs and cloud-edge artefacts live, and it is why an all-urban province like DKI Jakarta was reporting burned area at all.
  3. Accumulation — over the selected period, every pixel that fired at least once is kept. A pixel burning for five days running is one burned pixel, not five.
  4. Forest test — a burned pixel counts as forest when the in-house Random-Forest land-cover map (2025, 30 m) says forest at that pixel's centre.

2 · From detections to burned area

Burned area is the summed area of the distinct cells that fired — and each cell's area is measured, not assumed:

$$\text{burned area}\;[\mathrm{km^2}] \;=\; \sum_{\text{distinct cells}} \text{pixelArea}$$

A cell is nominally 375 m × 375 m = 0.140625 km², and multiplying a count by that constant is the obvious shortcut. It is also a guess about a grid nobody checked: these products are swath rasters — rotated, and not necessarily 375 m in whatever projection the reduction ends up running in. So the tool sums ee.Image.pixelArea per Kabupaten instead, and keeps the constant only as a fallback.

Each city gets two figures — total burned area and the part of it that was forest — plus the share of its own forest that has gone. The forest denominator is sampled rather than summed: one 30 m reading stands for each 250 m cell. That is unbiased for an area total and costs a fraction of reading all 2.1 billion 30 m pixels of Indonesia, which would be a batch export rather than a dashboard.

4 · Limits to keep in mind

  • This is a hotspot footprint, not a burn perimeter. One smouldering hectare lights its whole 375 m cell — all 14 ha of it — so the total typically runs several times the burned area a ministry maps from Landsat.
  • It runs short where cloud hid a fire at every overpass. Thermal sees through smoke, but not through thick cloud.
  • No burn perimeter or severity on this screen — that is the burn-scar detector's job (§5). It was rebuilt on Sentinel-1 radar on 2026-10-17 precisely because the fire season is the cloud and smoke season: an optical before-and-after pair over a month-long window in Kalimantan is mostly cloud holes.
  • Forest area is sampled rather than summed, so the forest-share column is noisiest on the smallest kota.
  • The LANCE feed publishes a day or more behind, so the daily chart stops at the last day published rather than at today.

5 · Burn-scar mapping after the season — Sentinel-1 time series

02_event/05_burnscar_detection.js maps what actually burned, once the season is over or well under way. It has the same panel and export shape as the landslide detector but a different sensor and a different kind of decision: radar, because cloud and smoke do not matter to it, and a time series rather than a single before/after pair, because one wet date reverses the burn signal and a pair cannot tell the two apart.

  1. One relative orbit per pixel. The Sentinel-1 orbits that cover at least 5 % of the AOI are ranked by coverage and season dates (up to three); each runs the whole chain and the results are mosaicked by rank — the primary orbit wherever it observed, the next only in its gaps. A pixel never mixes viewing geometries. The baseline is the mean of the acquisitions in the before window: two months ahead of the season start by default, dry and before any burning.
  2. Per acquisition, the drop from baseline — \( \Delta\mathrm{VH}_d = \mathrm{VH}_d - \mathrm{VH}_{\text{baseline}} \) in dB, after a 50 m focal mean. Burned ground is darker in VH.
  3. Rain confidence per date. CHIRPS rain over the three days before the acquisition gives each date a weight: 1 at ≤ 5 mm, falling linearly to 0.1 at ≥ 40 mm. A date counts as dry at weight ≥ 0.5. A second, radar-only gate catches what the rain product misses: when the AOI-wide median drop is positive by more than 0.3 dB, wet soil has brightened everything and the date is demoted whatever CHIRPS says.
  4. Onset — the first dry date with \( \Delta\mathrm{VH} < -2 \) dB.
  5. Burned — the rain-weighted mean drop over the dates at and after onset is below −1.5 dB; patches ≥ 1 ha; open water excluded (WorldCover water objects ≥ 50 ha, or baseline VV < −17 dB — without this a wind-roughened sea fills the map).
  6. Status — the number of dry confirmations (drop < −1 dB at or after onset): 1 candidate · 2 provisional · 3+ confirmed. Areas are reported by status and the confirmed figure is the one to quote; the first observation alone is wrong about half the time.

The thresholds were fixed on Central Kalimantan 2019 and then held fixed on four other sites against the Gaveau et al. Sentinel-2 burn maps: Dice 0.77 (Central Kalimantan 2019), 0.67 (OKI), 0.65 (Ketapang), 0.73 (Jambi), and 0.52 in the 2023 season when only 2 % of the land burned, at the same 3 % false-alarm rate. An unsupervised rule, equal to a random forest trained on the reference maps. Those reference maps themselves omit about a quarter of burns, which is why these figures are reported here but not in the project's frozen parameter record.

Outputs

Event ids carry the fire_ prefix. Fire is area only: there is no Stage 2 and no dashboard entry, and the area table is the deliverable. Always read observed_pct next to the hectares — a district the primary orbit barely saw cannot show much burned area, whatever happened on the ground.

Limits

  • Fires before the baseline window are invisible; an annual reference map will count them as misses.
  • Cropland harvest produces the same VH drop and still passes (commission 0.5–0.9 on cropland).
  • A single observation has commission around 0.5; three dry observations are needed, which on one orbit means roughly a month after onset.
  • CHIRPS publishes one to two months behind. Dates it has not covered keep full weight unless the radar self-gate rejects them.
