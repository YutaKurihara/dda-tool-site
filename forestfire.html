Forest Fire Monitoring and Burned Area Mapping

The tool uses satellite observations to monitor active fires and to estimate burned areas across Indonesia. It provides two complementary functions. Active Fire Monitoring (the screen above) uses VIIRS thermal data to identify locations where active fires have been detected; the results support fire monitoring and help identify areas requiring attention. Burned Area Mapping uses Sentinel-1 radar time-series data to identify vegetation changes associated with burning and to estimate burned area by district. The two methods provide different information and should not be interpreted as equivalent measurements.

Important: active fire detections are not burned area. VIIRS detects thermal activity within satellite observation cells. The full area of a detected cell does not necessarily represent land that has burned — a single smouldering hectare lights a whole 375 m cell, all 14 ha of it. The active-fire footprint should therefore be used to monitor fire activity, not as an estimate of total burned area. For burned area estimates, use the Sentinel-1 Burned Area Mapping function (below).

Part A · Active Fire Monitoring

What it provides

  • Daily map of active-fire detections for all 514 Kabupaten/Kota, ranked by province.
  • Per district: the active-fire detection footprint, the part of it within mapped forest, and the share of the district's forest covered by detections.
  • A daily chart of newly detected area within a live 30-day window, or any month of the last six years.

How it works

  1. Read the thermal feed. VIIRS near-real-time active fire detections at 375 m from both platforms, SNPP and NOAA-20, which roughly doubles the overpasses per day.
  2. Keep confident detections. Only nominal- and high-confidence detections count; the low-confidence class is where gas flares, hot industrial roofs and cloud-edge artefacts live.
  3. Accumulate over the period. Every cell that fired at least once in the selected period is kept once; a cell burning for five days is one cell, not five.
  4. Split by forest. A detected cell counts as forest when the in-house land-cover map (2025, 30 m) says forest at its centre.

Naming of the figures

The screen's short captions should be read with the following meanings. They describe footprints of thermal detections, not land that has burned.

Accuracy and limitations

  • The footprint is a hotspot footprint, not a burn perimeter. It typically runs several times larger than the burned area a ministry maps from Landsat.
  • It runs short where cloud hid a fire at every overpass: thermal sees through smoke, but not through thick cloud.
  • Forest area is sampled rather than summed, so the forest-share column is noisiest on the smallest kota.
  • The LANCE feed publishes a day or more behind, so the daily chart stops at the last day published rather than at today.
  • No burn severity, no perimeter and no damage estimate on this screen.
Technical details — Active Fire Monitoring

Script: 03_dashboard/02_forestfire.js. Sources NASA/LANCE/SNPP_VIIRS/C2 and NASA/LANCE/NOAA20_VIIRS/C2, filtered to Indonesia. A pixel counts when Bright_ti4 > 0 and the detection is of nominal or high confidence. The footprint is the summed area of the distinct cells that fired — and each cell's area is measured, not assumed:

$$\text{active-fire footprint}\;[\mathrm{km^2}] \;=\; \sum_{\text{distinct cells}} \text{pixelArea}$$

A cell is nominally 375 m × 375 m = 0.140625 km², and multiplying a count by that constant is the obvious shortcut. It is also a guess about a grid nobody checked: these products are swath rasters — rotated, and not necessarily 375 m in whatever projection the reduction ends up running in. So the tool sums ee.Image.pixelArea per Kabupaten instead, and keeps the constant only as a fallback. The forest denominator is sampled rather than summed: one 30 m reading stands for each 250 m cell, unbiased for an area total at a fraction of the cost of reading all 2.1 billion 30 m pixels of Indonesia.

Part B · Burned Area Mapping

What it provides

  • A 30 m map of estimated burned area for a fire season, with a detection status for every patch (candidate / provisional / confirmed by algorithm).
  • Burned hectares per Kabupaten/Kota, by status, together with the share of each district the radar actually observed.
  • Preliminary direct physical damage for the burned area, through the same damage estimation as the other hazards.

How it works

  1. Establish pre-fire conditions. Sentinel-1 radar observations from the two months before the fire season are used to establish reference conditions for every pixel.
  2. Detect radar changes. On each new observation the tool identifies areas where radar backscatter has dropped in a manner consistent with vegetation loss caused by fire.
  3. Account for rainfall. Rainfall information (CHIRPS) and a radar-based check are used to reduce false detections caused by wet conditions, which can mask or reverse the burn signal.
  4. Confirm persistent changes. Multiple dry-condition observations are used to distinguish persistent changes from temporary fluctuations; the number of confirmations is the detection status.
  5. Estimate burned area. The detected areas are mapped and summarised by district, and — for provisional and confirmed patches — passed to the damage estimation.

Burned area detection status

Candidate 1 dry-condition confirmation. Early indication; high uncertainty — about half of candidate area is later not confirmed.
Provisional 2 confirmations. Additional observation is recommended.
Confirmed by algorithm 3 or more confirmations. Higher confidence, but not field-verified.

Confirmed means confirmed by the algorithm from repeated satellite observations. It does not mean that burning was verified on the ground. Quote the confirmed figure; the candidate layer is the first-observation picture and roughly half of it is false at that point.

Validation

The burned area detection method was tested in several regions of Indonesia using independent satellite-derived burned area reference maps (Sentinel-2, Gaveau et al.). Agreement with the reference maps varied by region and year, with Dice scores ranging from 0.52 to 0.77 in the reported tests (Central Kalimantan 2019 0.77, Jambi 0.73, OKI 0.67, Ketapang 0.65, Central Kalimantan 2023 0.52 — a year in which only 2 % of the land burned, at the same 3 % false-alarm rate as 2019). A higher Dice score indicates better spatial agreement between the detected burned areas and the reference map. Accuracy depends on land cover, rainfall conditions, observation availability and the completeness of the reference data, which itself omits about a quarter of burns.

Recent observations. Rainfall data may not be available for the most recent satellite observations: CHIRPS publishes one to two months behind. When rainfall information is unavailable, the tool relies more heavily on radar-based checks. Recent burned area estimates may therefore carry additional uncertainty until more observations become available.

Damage estimation

Since 2026-10-17 a burned-area event goes through the same damage estimation and dashboard as the other hazards, once both the burned-area map and its status raster have landed. The estimate covers provisional and confirmed patches only, assumes total loss of the assets inside them, and leaves out roads, power lines and annual cropland — the first because a burn scar crossing a road does not destroy the road, the last because the radar cannot tell a burned field from a harvested one. Oil palm, forest, housing, facilities and commerce are valued. These figures are uncalibrated: no fire event has yet been compared with a reported loss. Read them as burned area × replacement cost.

Common rule for landslides, earthquakes and fires. For preliminary damage estimation, assets within the detected area are assumed to be completely destroyed. Actual damage must be verified separately.

Accuracy and limitations

  • Cropland harvest produces the same radar drop as burning and is often detected as burned (commission 0.5–0.9 on cropland). Read burned-area figures on agricultural land with caution.
  • Fires before the reference window are invisible; an annual reference map will count them as misses.
  • A single observation has commission around 0.5; three dry observations are needed for a confirmed status, which on one Sentinel-1 orbit means roughly a month after onset.
  • Districts the primary orbit barely observed cannot show much burned area whatever happened on the ground — always read observed_pct next to the hectares.
  • The damage estimate is preliminary and uncalibrated (see above).
Technical details — Burned Area Mapping

Script: 02_event/05_burnscar_detection.js. Same panel and export shape as the landslide detector, but radar — cloud and smoke do not matter to it — and a time series rather than a single before/after pair, because one wet date reverses the burn signal and a pair cannot tell the two apart.

  1. One relative orbit per pixel. The Sentinel-1 orbits that cover at least 5 % of the AOI are ranked by coverage and season dates (up to three); each runs the whole chain and the results are mosaicked by rank — the primary orbit wherever it observed, the next only in its gaps. A pixel never mixes viewing geometries. The baseline is the mean of the acquisitions in the before window: two months ahead of the season start by default, dry and before any burning.
  2. Per acquisition, the drop from baseline — \( \Delta\mathrm{VH}_d = \mathrm{VH}_d - \mathrm{VH}_{\text{baseline}} \) in dB, after a 50 m focal mean. Burned ground is darker in VH.
  3. Rain confidence per date. CHIRPS rain over the three days before the acquisition gives each date a weight: 1 at ≤ 5 mm, falling linearly to 0.1 at ≥ 40 mm. A date counts as dry at weight ≥ 0.5. A second, radar-only gate catches what the rain product misses: when the AOI-wide median drop is positive by more than 0.3 dB, wet soil has brightened everything and the date is demoted whatever CHIRPS says. Dates CHIRPS has not yet published keep full weight unless this gate rejects them.
  4. Onset — the first dry date with \( \Delta\mathrm{VH} < -2 \) dB.
  5. Burned — the rain-weighted mean drop over the dates at and after onset is below −1.5 dB; patches ≥ 1 ha; open water excluded (WorldCover water objects ≥ 50 ha, or baseline VV < −17 dB — without this a wind-roughened sea fills the map).
  6. Status — the number of dry confirmations (drop < −1 dB at or after onset): 1 candidate · 2 provisional · 3+ confirmed.

The thresholds were fixed on Central Kalimantan 2019 and then held fixed on four other sites against the Gaveau et al. Sentinel-2 burn maps (Dice above). An unsupervised rule, equal to a random forest trained on the reference maps. Because those reference maps omit about a quarter of burns, the figures are reported here but not in the project's frozen parameter record.
