Landslide extent

A landslide strips vegetation and exposes bare soil or rock. The detector (02_event/02_landslide_detection.js) finds this signature in Sentinel-2 optical imagery as a sharp drop in vegetation index, confirmed by a bare-soil or water signal.

Detection logic

  1. Image pairs — cloud-free Sentinel-2 median composites are built on either side of the event date, with deliberately unequal windows: a long one before (six months by default — a stable, fully vegetated reference) and a short one after (two months). A scar in the tropics greens up within weeks, so a long post-event median is dominated by scenes in which the slide has already been recolonised and the change disappears. Clouds are masked per pixel with Google's Cloud Score+ dataset, which is far more robust in the humid tropics than classic QA bitmasks.
  2. Vegetation loss — a pixel is a candidate when NDVI drops by more than 0.25 and the pre-event NDVI was above 0.5 (it must have been vegetated to begin with — this suppresses false alarms on fields and settlements).
  3. Surface confirmation — the candidate is kept only if the post-event surface looks like a slide scar: bare soil (BSI > 0.05) or newly wet/ponded ground (MNDWI > 0 with low NIR reflectance).
  4. Cleanup — a minimum-cluster filter (≥ 5 pixels ≈ 500 m²) removes fragments smaller than any damaging slide. There is no morphological erosion: a 3 × 3 focal_min deletes every slide narrower than three pixels — 30 m at export, 90 m in the coarse preview — and shallow slides here are often 10–40 m wide.
  5. Terrain gate, by patch — a connected patch is kept only if some pixel of it lies on ground steeper than 10° (HydroSHEDS 90 m). This is deliberately not a pixel mask: a slide starts on the hillside and comes to rest on the valley floor, the lake shore or the road, and that deposit is where the damage is. Masking by slope pixel by pixel would keep the scarp and delete the damage. The patch test keeps the whole slide and drops a harvest, a clearing or a drained pond on flat land, which have no steep pixel anywhere. Curvature is not used; at 30–90 m it is noise.

From extent to damage

Unlike a flood, a landslide has no depth gradient that maps onto a depth–damage curve — anything caught in the slide mass or scar is effectively destroyed. The pipeline encodes this with a simple convention:

Total-loss convention: the detector exports only a mask (landslidemask_<id>, no depth asset). The valuation engine synthesises a constant 10 m depth inside the mask; every damage curve saturates at 100 % well before 10 m, so all exposed assets inside the extent are valued at full reconstruction cost. No special-case code paths exist downstream — Stage 2 and the dashboard are byte-for-byte the same as for floods.

Limits to keep in mind

  • Optical detection needs at least one usable clear scene after the event — in persistent cloud the map is delayed until one exists.
  • Slides under intact forest canopy, or smaller than ~500 m², are not detected.
  • The event id carries the landslide_ prefix; the engine and dashboard route the total-loss convention from this prefix alone.
