Landslide Detection and Impact Assessment

The tool uses Sentinel-2 satellite images to identify potential landslide areas by detecting vegetation loss and exposed soil after a disaster. The detected areas are combined with road, building and infrastructure data to identify potentially affected assets and estimate direct physical damage. The results can help identify areas requiring field inspection and road sections that may be blocked by landslides.

What the tool provides

  • Maps of potential landslide-affected areas.
  • Locations where detected landslides intersect national, regional and toll roads — potential road blockages.
  • Estimates of exposed buildings, infrastructure and people.
  • Preliminary estimates of direct physical damage.

How it works

  1. Compare satellite images. Cloud-free Sentinel-2 composites before and after the disaster are built over equal windows, so that both sides are estimated the same way.
  2. Detect surface changes. Areas showing a large decrease in vegetation and an increase in exposed soil (or newly ponded ground) are identified as potential landslides.
  3. Remove unlikely detections. Clouds, small isolated changes and ground that was not vegetated to begin with are excluded.
  4. Assess potential impacts. The detected areas are overlaid with buildings, roads and infrastructure to estimate potential damage and to identify road sections requiring verification.

Damage estimation

The damage model assumes that assets located within detected landslide areas are completely destroyed. Their damage is therefore estimated using 100 % of the applicable replacement cost. This is a simplified assumption for preliminary assessment: actual damage may vary and should be verified using field observations.

Common rule for landslides, earthquakes and fires. For preliminary damage estimation, assets within the detected area are assumed to be completely destroyed. Actual damage must be verified separately.

Potential road blockages

The tool identifies locations where detected landslide areas intersect national, regional or toll roads. These locations indicate possible road blockages and can help prioritise field inspections. They are a satellite-detected overlap, not a confirmed closure: actual road conditions, including whether a road remains passable, must be confirmed by local authorities. On the dashboard they are drawn as × markers, each with the length of road affected and the road name.

Accuracy and limitations

  • The method requires usable cloud-free satellite images before and after the event; in persistent cloud the map is delayed until one exists.
  • Landslides hidden beneath intact vegetation may not be detected.
  • Small landslides (below about 500 m²) may be missed.
  • Vegetation loss caused by logging, agriculture or other activities may be incorrectly classified as landslides.
  • Road blockage locations and damage estimates require field verification.

Outputs

AssetContent
landslidemask_<event_id>Binary landslide extent, 10 m (band flooded, shared schema)
roadblock_<event_id>One point per contiguous stretch where the extent crosses a national, regional or toll road, with the metres of road affected and the road name — a possible blockage, drawn × on the dashboard

Both tasks are queued at once. If the slide touches no road, the blockage task fails with "Table is empty" — Earth Engine cannot write an empty table to an Asset — and the dashboard simply omits the × layer. That is a normal result, not an error.

Technical details

For analysts. The detector is 02_event/02_landslide_detection.js.

T1 · Detection logic

  1. Image pairs — cloud-free Sentinel-2 median composites are built over equal windows either side of the event date (six months each by default), so that both sides are estimated the same way. The after window can be shortened when a scar has visibly greened up again in the after composite. Clouds are masked per pixel with Google's Cloud Score+ dataset, which is far more robust in the humid tropics than classic QA bitmasks.
  2. Vegetation loss — a pixel is a candidate when NDVI drops by more than 0.25 and the pre-event NDVI was above 0.5 (it must have been vegetated to begin with — this suppresses false alarms on fields and settlements).
  3. Surface confirmation — the candidate is kept only if the post-event surface looks like a slide scar: bare soil (BSI > 0.05) or newly wet/ponded ground (MNDWI > 0 with low NIR reflectance).
  4. Cleanup — a minimum-cluster filter (≥ 5 pixels ≈ 500 m²) removes fragments smaller than any damaging slide. There is no morphological erosion: a 3 × 3 focal_min deletes every slide narrower than three pixels — 30 m at export, 90 m in the coarse preview — and shallow slides here are often 10–40 m wide.

T2 · How the total-loss rule is implemented

Unlike a flood, a landslide has no depth gradient that maps onto a depth–damage curve. The detector exports only a mask (landslidemask_<id>, no depth asset). The valuation engine synthesises a constant 10 m depth inside the mask; every damage curve saturates at 100 % well before 10 m, so all exposed assets inside the extent are valued at full reconstruction cost. No special-case code paths exist downstream — Stage 2 and the dashboard are the same as for floods. The event id carries the landslide_ prefix; the engine and dashboard route the total-loss convention from this prefix alone.
