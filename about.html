Satellite-based Disaster Damage Assessment for DIBI / Jitupasna

A Google Earth Engine tool that detects the footprint of floods, landslides and earthquakes from Sentinel-1/2 imagery, and converts it into monetary damage (IDR) following the Jitupasna post-disaster needs-assessment guideline (Perka BNPB No.15/2011) and the reporting items of BNPB's DIBI database.

Everything runs on Google Earth Engine — no local GIS software is needed to produce an estimate. Heavy computation is done once per event as a batch export; the dashboard only reads the saved results, so it opens in seconds.

How the pipeline works

Stage 1 — Detection One script per hazard: flood (Sentinel-1 change detection + water depth), landslide (Sentinel-2 vegetation loss), earthquake (RV-PWTT building collapse). Output: a 10 m hazard mask (+ depth raster for floods).
→
Stage 2 — Valuation One shared damage engine overlays the hazard on 37 exposure layers (buildings, roads, facilities, land use, population) and prices the damage pixel by pixel. Output: one table per event (damage_value_<id>).
→
Stage 3 — Reporting The dashboard maps the results per district (kabupaten/kota) and exports two CSVs: DIBI (wide, one row per district) and Jitupasna (long, district × damage class).

The three stages communicate only through Earth Engine Assets, so adding a new hazard detector requires no change to the valuation engine or the dashboard — this is exactly how landslide and earthquake support were added.

How damage is priced

For every damage class (housing, health facilities, roads, rice fields, …) the engine evaluates, at each 10 m pixel:

$$\text{damage}_{\text{IDR}} \;=\; \sum_{\text{pixels}} \Big[\, \text{quantity} \times \text{unit cost}_{100\%} \times \mathrm{DDF}(\text{depth}) \,\Big] \times \text{province ratio}$$
  • Quantity — flooded area (m² or ha) or length (km) of the exposed asset in that pixel.
  • Unit cost — the full reconstruction cost at 100 % damage, compiled from Indonesian government price standards (DKI SHST 2025, HSPK, PUPR) and statistics. Every value is tagged with its source.
  • DDF — depth–damage functions (JRC, Huizinga et al. 2017, Asia set): the fraction of value lost at a given water depth. Evaluated per pixel, not on a district average, because the curves are non-linear.
  • Province ratio — regional construction-cost index (BPS IKK 2025, DKI Jakarta = 1.00).

Landslides and earthquakes have no meaningful "depth", so the engine assigns a constant saturating depth inside the detected extent — every curve reaches 100 % loss, i.e. anything inside the footprint is treated as destroyed.

Beyond monetary damage the same run also produces the Sendai Framework (SFDRR) indicators: affected facility counts (Target D), damaged / destroyed housing split (C-4), and exposed / affected population from WorldPop (B-1, B-3, B-4).
