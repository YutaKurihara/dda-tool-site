Data Quality and Damage Estimation Verification

This page explains how the DDA Tool checks the data and calculation methods used to estimate disaster damage. Damage estimates depend on the quality of the input data, unit costs and assumptions used in the calculation. The Data Check screen above helps users review these inputs, identify missing data and verify the damage calculation process. It reads constants and Assets only, performs no batch computation and writes nothing.

What can be checked

  • Exposure data — buildings, infrastructure, agricultural land and other assets used in damage assessment, with the producing ministry and the feature counts.
  • Unit costs — replacement costs at 100 % damage and their original sources.
  • Damage functions — the relationships used to estimate damage from flood depth.
  • Regional adjustments — differences in construction costs across provinces.
  • Data availability — whether every required dataset is present.
  • Calculation tests — checks that the damage model produces the expected results on known cases.
Understanding data quality. Passing the data checks confirms that the required datasets are available and that selected calculations behave as expected. It does not guarantee that all input datasets are current, complete or accurate. Users should review data sources, update dates, spatial resolution and known limitations before using damage estimates in official assessments.

Sections of the screen

SectionContent
Unit CostsThe 100 %-damage reconstruction cost of every class, tagged with provenance: [G] statistics-based, [E] recovery-budget request, [Furukawa] expert proposal — traceable to dataset_inventory.xlsx and the government source PDFs.
DDF CurvesAll 13 depth–damage functions plotted from their control points. Four are transcribed from the JRC Asia tables (Huizinga et al. 2017); the rest are tagged as unsourced in the code.
Province RatioThe BPS IKK 2025 construction-cost index per province (DKI Jakarta = 1.00).
ExposureThe 38 exposure assets with their producing ministry, feature counts and DIBI column, plus the reference datasets — BIG boundaries (province to village), the 2025 land cover, the BNPB InaRISK population raster. Verify every Asset probes all 44 source Assets and reports each as present or NOT FOUND.
Sanity TestA worked example — one house in Jakarta flooded to 1.5 m — computed live, verifiable by hand.
Engine TestPixel-wise vs. scalar DDF agreement, a hand-calculated damage case, the population scale test (InaRISK is a 30 m people-per-pixel grid; reducing it at 10 m would inflate population nine-fold), and old-vs-new engine totals.
